Cybersecurity Dictionary

72-hour breach notification

The requirement to notify the NPC (and affected people, when required) within 72 hours of learning about a qualifying breach.

In the storyThe clock started Tuesday at 9:20 a.m., and two days were spent waiting in a vendor's ticket queue.Think of it likeLike reporting a lost ATM card right away. Every hour you wait, the damage can grow.

Cloud service

Computing (servers, storage, software) rented from a provider over the internet instead of run in your own building.

In the storyAfter the typhoon, South Bridgee moved the grading portal to the cloud so outages would be "someone else's storage."Think of it likeLike renting a condo instead of building a house. Someone else fixes the elevator, but it is still your stuff inside.

Complementary User Entity Controls (CUECs)

Controls the customer must do itself for the vendor's controls to work, listed inside the SOC 2 report.

In the storyThe first CUEC was "Enable multi-factor authentication for all administrator accounts." South Bridgee had not.Think of it likeLike a warranty that says "keep away from water." If you don't, the protection does not hold.

Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)

The Philippine law that protects personal information and sets duties for organizations that handle it.

In the storyUnder the Data Privacy Act, South Bridgee stayed responsible even after outsourcing.Think of it likeIt is the law behind the privacy notices you tick when you register for school, banks or apps.

Data Protection Officer (DPO)

The person responsible for the organization's compliance with data privacy law.

In the storyAtty. Grace Villanueva, the DPO, read the contract and the SOC 2 report out loud for the first time.Think of it likeLike a school's guidance office for data: the person who knows the rules and who must be informed.

Data return and deletion (exit clause)

Rules for getting your data back, in a usable format, and proof of deletion when the contract ends.

In the storyKalinaw's terms said data "may be deleted" 30 days after termination, "in no particular format."Think of it likeLike moving out of a boarding house: you want your things back in boxes, not "we might throw them out."

Due diligence

Checking a vendor's evidence and risks before signing, not after something goes wrong.

In the storyThis week's lesson: "due diligence before the contract."Think of it likeLike checking reviews, the address and the business permit before paying a deposit.

Failover

Automatically switching to a backup system or data center when the main one fails.

In the storyKalinaw advertised "automatic failover across two data centers," which protects availability, not confidentiality.Think of it likeLike a generator that turns on by itself when the power goes out.

Misconfigured cloud storage (open bucket)

Cloud storage set to "public" by mistake, so anyone on the internet can read the files.

In the storyThe subcontractor "left a reporting bucket open to the internet" with 4,000 students' grades in it.Think of it likeLike posting a photo album as Public instead of Only Me.

Multi-factor authentication (MFA)

Logging in with two or more proofs: something you know (password) plus something you have (phone code) or are.

In the storyKalinaw expected South Bridgee to turn on MFA for admin accounts; it never did.Think of it likeLike the one-time PIN your bank or e-wallet texts you after you type your password.

National Privacy Commission (NPC)

The Philippine government agency that enforces the Data Privacy Act and receives breach notifications.

In the storyThe duty to notify the NPC belonged to South Bridgee, not to Kalinaw.Think of it likeThe office you can complain to if an app or company mishandles your personal data.

Personal data breach

Personal data that was exposed, stolen, changed or lost because security failed, no matter whose system it was on.

In the storyKalinaw said its platform "had not been breached." The data was still breached, through its subcontractor.Think of it likeLike saying "our store wasn't robbed" when your delivery rider lost the package. It is still your customer's loss.

Personal information controller (PIC)

The organization that decides why and how personal data is processed. It stays accountable for it.

In the storySouth Bridgee "was still the personal information controller" of the student records.Think of it likeLike a parent who hires a babysitter. The babysitter helps, but the parent is still responsible.

Personal information processor (PIP)

A company that processes personal data on the controller's behalf and instructions, like a cloud vendor.

In the storyKalinaw and its subcontractor "only processed the data on its behalf."Think of it likeLike a photocopy shop: it handles your documents, but they are still yours.

Procurement

The organization's process for choosing and buying a product or service.

In the storyProcurement took three weeks, but nobody read the evidence the vendor sent.Think of it likeLike buying a used phone: the buying steps are done, but did anyone check the IMEI?

Right to audit

A contract clause that lets the customer (or an auditor) check the vendor's security controls and evidence.

In the storyThe contract "gave South Bridgee no right to audit anything."Think of it likeLike a landlord letting you inspect the unit before renewing. No clause, no inspection.

Scheduled maintenance exclusion

Downtime the vendor announces in advance and does not count against its availability promise.

In the storyThe 99.9% promise excluded scheduled maintenance, so planned outages did not count at all.Think of it likeLike a "brownout schedule" announcement: the power is off, but it does not count as a failure.

Security questionnaire

A list of security questions a customer sends a vendor. Answers are claims; they need evidence behind them.

In the storyKalinaw answered "Yes" beside every item, with no proof attached.Think of it likeLike a seller writing "no issues" on a secondhand laptop. You still test it before paying.

Security researcher

A person who looks for security weaknesses and reports them so they can be fixed.

In the storyA researcher scanning for misconfigured storage found the exposed grades file and emailed South Bridgee.Think of it likeLike a neighbor who notices your gate is open and texts you.

Sensitive personal information

Personal data that needs extra protection under the law, including information about a person's education, health, and government IDs.

In the storyStudents' grades are about their education, so the records counted as sensitive personal information.Think of it likeLike your report card and medical records: things you would not want posted publicly.

Service credit

A discount the vendor gives when it misses an SLA. Usually small, and usually the only remedy.

In the storyMissing the SLA earned South Bridgee "five percent of that month's fee," nothing for a data breach.Think of it likeLike a 5% voucher after the store lost your order. It does not bring the order back.

Service-Level Agreement (SLA)

The part of a contract that sets measurable service promises and what happens when they are missed.

In the storyKalinaw's SLA covered only availability, not breach reporting, subcontractors, audits or data return.Think of it likeLike a delivery app promising "30 minutes or a voucher." It says nothing about a wrong or missing order.

Shared responsibility

The split of security duties between customer and provider. Each side must do its part.

In the story"A clear line on who is responsible for what" was missing between South Bridgee and Kalinaw.Think of it likeLike a rented apartment: the owner fixes the roof, but you lock your own door.

SOC 2 report

An independent auditor's report on a service provider's controls, based on the AICPA Trust Services Criteria.

In the storyEveryone found Kalinaw's SOC 2 "reassuring," but nobody read past the cover page.Think of it likeLike a restaurant's sanitation inspection result. Useful only if you read what was inspected and when.

SOC 2 Type 1 vs Type 2

Type 1 checks whether controls were designed well on one date. Type 2 tests whether they actually worked over a period, often 6–12 months.

In the storyKalinaw's report was a Type 1 from twenty months earlier: a snapshot of design, not proof of operation.Think of it likeType 1 is like checking that a gym has a fire extinguisher. Type 2 is checking it was inspected and working all year.

Standard terms of service

The vendor's own default contract. It is written to protect the vendor, not the customer.

In the storySomeone accepted Kalinaw's standard terms "with a click," so no South Bridgee protections were added.Think of it likeLike tapping "I agree" on an app without reading what it can do with your data.

Subcontractor (fourth party)

A company your vendor hires to do part of the work. You may not know it exists, but it may hold your data.

In the storyAn analytics company Kalinaw used, which South Bridgee "had never heard of," leaked the data.Think of it likeLike a delivery app passing your order to a rider you never chose.

Subservice organization (carve-out)

A vendor's own provider that the SOC 2 report leaves out. Its controls are not covered by the audit.

In the storyPage 12 said the report "excluded Kalinaw's subservice organizations," including the one that leaked the data.Think of it likeLike a school clearance that covers the canteen but not the outside caterer that actually cooks the food.

Support ticket and response time

A formal request logged with a vendor's support team. The contract decides how fast they must answer.

In the storyThe automatic reply promised a response "within three business days," while South Bridgee's 72-hour clock kept running.Think of it likeLike a courier hotline saying "we'll get back to you in 3–5 working days" when your package is urgent.

Trust Services Criteria

The five areas a SOC 2 can cover: Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality and Privacy.

In the storyKalinaw's report "covered the Security criterion only."Think of it likeLike a car inspection that checked only the brakes, not the lights or tires.

Uptime / availability (99.9%)

The share of time a service is working. 99.9% in a 30-day month still allows about 43 minutes of downtime.

In the storyKalinaw promised 99.9% uptime, and it was true. Availability was never the problem in this story.Think of it likeLike a jeepney route that runs 99.9% of the time. Reliable, but it says nothing about whether your bag is safe on it.
